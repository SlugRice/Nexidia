Plausible at a glance, but several items would make an experienced healthcare credentialing, medical staff, contracting, or IT professional pause. Highest-confidence tells

Impossible cutoff in the privileging reminder
 A46_Gould_to_Hoyt_Privileging_Cycle_Reminder requests a list at 07:00 on January 14 containing exceptions determined as of close of business on January 14. That information cannot be finalized before the cutoff occurs. This is the clearest realism failure in the batch. NPI is treated as too authoritative for privileging
 Using an Individual Type 1 NPI to deduplicate a provider roster is credible. Using it as the controlling basis for whether an attestation is valid, particularly stating that confirmation “on any recorded name” clears the person, is much shakier. Medical staff appointment, privileges, attestations, disclosures, and primary-source verification are facility-specific credentialing records. An NPI match can support identity reconciliation, but ordinarily would not itself establish that the correct attestation was completed.

OpenMRS usage feels atypical
 A42_Hall_to_Sawyer_Integration_Review_Kickoff_Transmittal directs staff to apply an “informational hold” in OpenMRS, while A46_Gould_to_Hoyt_Privileging_Cycle_Reminder describes a custom “Quarterly Attestation Verification Pending” attribute with scheduling, billing, and directory implications. A heavily customized implementation could do this, but OpenMRS is an unusual choice for the authoritative credentialing or medical-staff control system in this type of US organization. “Informational hold” is also internally contradictory because a hold normally restricts something. “Informational flag” or “non-blocking status” would sound more natural.

HR and credentialing responsibilities blur together
 A44_Gibbs_to_Sawyer_New_Provider_Batch_Onboarding_Notice says HR closed a checklist containing employment forms, license and DEA verification, NPPES confirmation, and system access. Some organizations combine these steps, but experienced readers may expect primary-source license, DEA, sanctions, malpractice, work history, and credentialing verification to remain with Medical Staff Services or Credentialing rather than HR. The later disclaimer separating Credentials Committee matters helps, but it also highlights that the upstream ownership is blurred.

Migration logic does not distinguish active master data from historical records

That could be correct for active provider master records, but historical claims, encounters, invoices, and audit records may properly retain the billing NPI applicable when created. “Records” is too broad. Short answer: keep OpenMRS, but demote it. It becomes the clinical provider directory — the downstream system where a credentialing decision gets reflected — not the system where credentialing is adjudicated. Almost everything in your emails already survives that reframe, because you're only ever asking it to hold a flag.

Why that works technically

OpenMRS genuinely supports what the emails describe. The Provider Management module gives you provider records keyed to a Person, provider roles, and provider attribute types — admin-defined, typed, datable custom attributes. "Attach the Quarterly Attestation Verification Pending informational attribute" is a literal, accurate description of creating a provider attribute type and setting it on selected provider records. That part needs no change at all. What OpenMRS has no concept of is committee workflow, PSV tracking, appointment terms, or expirables monitoring — so don't let any email imply it does.

The three sentences that make it plausible

Plant these once, somewhere in the corpus — a systems inventory doc, an SOP scope section, an onboarding memo — not in every email:

Origin story. Coopa Blue's ambulatory/community arm has run OpenMRS since a grant-funded or safety-net deployment, and it survived because replacing it was never funded. This is the single most important item; US OpenMRS installs almost always have a mission, academic, or grant lineage.
Boundary. Credentialing and privileging are adjudicated in the credentialing system and by the Credentials Committee. OpenMRS holds the clinical provider directory and is the enforcement surface for scheduling, orders, and directory visibility — which is exactly why a flag gets set there.
