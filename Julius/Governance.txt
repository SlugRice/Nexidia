Overall verdict

Internally consistent in several headline numbers, but the governance structure and strategic-plan content would cause experienced healthcare executives to pause. The strongest tells are an incomplete organization chart, unusual responsibility assignments, financial timing errors, and a strategic plan that reads more like a narrative evidence package than an actual board-level plan.

Highest-confidence concerns

FY2025 revenue timing is wrong
 A41_Strategic_Plan_FY2026_2028_Excerpt.docx says FY2025 runs through June 30, 2025 and that Winston’s March 3, 2025 acquisition contributed ten months of FY2025 revenue. March 3 through June 30 is approximately four months, not ten. This is the clearest factual error in the set.

The organization chart omits essential hospital leadership
 A39_Organization_Chart_FY2026.pdf shows the CEO, CCO, CMO, Controller, HR Director, IT Director, three Compliance staff, and facilities. It does not show a CFO, COO, CIO, CNO, hospital presidents or administrators, Revenue Cycle, Legal, Quality, Nursing, Medical Staff Services, or clinical-service leadership. Yet the strategic plan assigns initiatives to several of those missing executives. For a two-hospital, 215-bed system with approximately 1,800 employees, this would look materially incomplete unless explicitly labeled a selected governance chart.

Facilities have no visible management relationship
 The chart says facilities report to the CEO through an SVP of Operations, but that SVP does not appear anywhere in the chart. The facilities are displayed as boxes without named leaders or clear reporting lines.

Compliance owns too much credentialing operations
 The Director of Credentialing, Senior Ops Analyst handling credentialing and payer-enrollment discrepancies, and medical-staff governance coordinator all report under the Chief Compliance Officer. Compliance can oversee these functions, but placing routine credentialing, medical-staff governance, administrative holds, payer-enrollment reconciliation, and implementation of privilege decisions inside Compliance is atypical and raises independence and role-separation questions.

The Board Secretary chairs the Compliance Committee
 Kevin Caster is simultaneously Board Secretary, Medical Staff Governance Coordinator, and chair of the Compliance Committee, while reporting within the Office of Compliance and having a dotted line to the Board. That concentration of recordkeeping, governance coordination, and compliance-program leadership would invite conflict-of-interest and independence questions.

Integration sponsorship is unusual. The CCO and Controller jointly sponsor systems migration, payer enrollment, credentialing harmonization, contracts, and governance. CRPA resolves here — Capital Region Payers Alliance, named in A41 §2.4.1 — so withdraw that objection from my A45 review. The org chart is also the first document that cleanly ratifies the Senior Ops Analyst role A03 and A06 keep invoking. But the fiscal calendar and the executive structure both come apart in this directory.

The fiscal year breaks three documents

A41 §1.4 fixes FY2026 as 07/01/2025 – 06/30/2026. Consequences:

The Winston revenue math is wrong by a factor of two and a half. §3.1 says the FY2025 baseline "includes ten months of Winston Imaging subsidiary revenue (03/03/2025 close through 06/30/2025 fiscal year-end)". That span is under four months. The parenthetical contradicts the number in the same sentence.

A29's quarter-end list is now provably wrong. It extends close at March, June, and September. On a July–June year the quarter ends are September, December, March, and June — December is missing, and June is the annual close, which cannot be a two-day extension.

A45 is labeled the wrong quarter. The Controller calls the period through 3/31/2026 "Q1 2026". On this fiscal calendar that's Q3 FY2026. A controller reporting to a CCO would never use calendar-quarter labels against a June year-end.

The plan was adopted mid-cycle. Board resolution 01/2026 for a plan that started 07/01/2025, and §6.1.3 commits the Board to annual review "at its first-quarter meeting of each fiscal year" — July through September, not January.

A41 cites its own future. Rev. 01/2026, citing A31 (Rev. 04/2026) and an integration cost "through 3/31/2026". Fifth controlled document with a forward reference. The $2.4M also lands exactly on A45's reported actual — a January projection matching an April actual to the dollar.

Four executives exist in prose and nowhere on the chart

A41 assigns initiatives and risks to a Chief Financial Officer, Chief Information Officer, Chief Nursing Officer, and Chief Human Resources Officer. A29 routes sign-off to the CFO five times. The org chart's entire C-suite is CCO, CMO, Controller, HR Director, IT/Systems Director. Spindell reports to the CEO as Controller — so the CFO he releases the reporting package to doesn't exist, and the Controller is simultaneously joint Executive Sponsor of I-01 alongside the CCO while the CFO co-sponsors I-04.

The chart also references an "SVP / Operations" with no box, and the CEO is never named anywhere in the corpus.

Below the C-suite, A29 names an Assistant Controller, Accounting Manager, AP Manager, Treasury, Property Accounting, Consolidations, and Revenue Cycle; A04 is prepared by a "Medical Staff Office / Scheduling". None appear.

Medical staff governance is missing its apparatus

The chart's notes describe the Credentials Committee, MEC, and governing body, but none is drawn, and neither is a Chief of Staff / President of the Medical Staff, a Chief Nursing Officer, or a Quality/Risk function. For an Article 28 hospital the MEC is a real governance body with an elected physician leader; here it exists only as a routing step in a footnote.

Two structural oddities a surveyor would question:

The CMO chairs the Credentials Committee. That chair is normally an elected member of the organized medical staff. An employed executive chairing it blurs the self-governance line the bylaws framework in §4.4 depends on.
The CCO reports solely to the CEO, with no dotted line to the board or an audit committee. OIG compliance-program guidance treats direct board access as the core independence control. Meanwhile Kevin Caster — a CCO direct report — chairs the Compliance Committee, while also serving as Board Secretary and recording governing-body actions. That's the person who documents privileging decisions also chairing the committee that would investigate them.

Also: Credentialing reports into Compliance, not to the CMO or a Medical Staff Office. Internally consistent with A03's ownership, but unusual enough in practice to draw a second look.

None of A41's four board committees — Finance & Audit, Community & Mission, Quality, People & Culture — appear under the Board box.

Numbers that won't survive scrutiny

$412M on 215 licensed beds is roughly $1.9M per bed, against a typical community-hospital range closer to $1.0–1.5M. With ~1,800 employees that's ~$229K revenue per employee, also high. Not impossible with heavy outpatient volume, but the imaging subsidiary is only $18M, so the outpatient story doesn't carry it.

"249 credentialed providers" counts the eight imaging technologists as credentialed providers. A41 §4.1 compounds it: the Winston workforce "comprised 24 providers together with the operational, technologist, and administrative staff" — but a third of that 24 are the technologists.

§3.4's payer mix describes "approximately balanced shares of Medicare, Medicaid, commercial, and self-pay-and-other". That puts self-pay near 25%. Real hospital self-pay runs low single digits. No CFO would write that sentence.

The Winston NPI contradiction gets a patch, not a fix

A41's milestone language — retirement of the legacy billing identifier "for internal billing purposes" — is the corpus's attempt to reconcile the conflict I flagged earlier. It almost works. But A31 states flatly that Winston retains its "legal entity, TIN, and organizational NPI", as does A03 §2.2, while A09 treats records carrying that NPI as remediation exceptions. A subsidiary that keeps its own TIN and NPI bills under them; there is no coherent state where the identifier is both retained and retired.

Separately, A31 uses "TIN" and A41 uses "EIN" for the same thing in parallel sentences.

Nonprofit-specific gaps

A three-year strategic plan for a New York 501(c)(3) hospital system with a whole initiative on community partnerships, and no mention of the Community Health Needs Assessment or the §501(r) implementation strategy, no charity care / financial assistance policy, no community benefit reporting, no 340B, and no Article 28 or DOH CON considerations. The CHNA is the single most conspicuous omission — it's a triennial federal requirement and it's what a community-partnership initiative would normally be anchored to.
