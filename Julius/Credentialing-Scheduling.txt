Overall verdict

The schedule is broadly recognizable, but it looks more like a simplified test fixture than a working healthcare coverage schedule. The biggest tells are incomplete coverage, missing operational fields, ambiguous overnight/on-call times, and specialty-to-assignment mismatches.

Highest-confidence concerns

It is not a complete hospital coverage schedule
 The grid contains selected hospitalists, clinics, OR assignments, imaging coverage, and call assignments, but many departments have no continuous coverage representation. Emergency Medicine appears only on two overnight shifts, while there is no corresponding daytime ED coverage. If this is intentionally a partial schedule, the title and scope do not say so.

Urgent Care physicians are assigned to Emergency Medicine
 Wallace T. Kimura is listed with specialty “Urgent Care Medicine” but assigned primary overnight ED coverage. That is possible if appropriately credentialed, but the schedule contains no indication of emergency privileges, board eligibility, or approved cross-coverage. An experienced hospital scheduler could pause here.

On-call assignments have no actual time range
 On-call entries use dashes for both start and end time, including entries described as 24-hour, home call, trauma call, and teleradiology. A real operational schedule normally needs precise handoff times because calendar dates alone leave midnight coverage ambiguous.

Overnight shifts do not identify the ending date
 A shift shown as 19:00 to 07:00 appears to end earlier than it starts unless the system explicitly interprets it as next-day coverage. There is no end-date or overnight indicator.

The grid mixes incompatible scheduling levels
 It combines physician shifts, clinic blocks, OR rooms, cath-lab assignments, rounding coverage, home call, teleradiology, and technologist call in one flat table. This can be a consolidated view, but the file lacks service-level rules needed to interpret each assignment type consistently.

Domain plausibility issues
Kathleen R. Chambers has an Internal Medicine specialty but is assigned to Family Medicine. That could be legitimate cross-coverage, but no explanation or privilege indicator is provided.
Katherine M. Castellano is listed as Family Medicine but works in the Internal Medicine Clinic on April 21. Again possible, but it contributes to a pattern of interchangeable clinic assignments.
Larry J. Farkas shifts between CBRMC visiting coverage and Coopa Blue North. A real schedule might identify home facility, temporary-privilege status, travel coverage, or cross-site authorization.
Shirley R. Marchetti, an RDMS ultrasound technologist, is included alongside medical-staff physician coverage and identified by an Individual NPI. This repeats the earlier questionable treatment of imaging technologists as individually enumerated providers.
“Hospitalist Nocturnist” is used as a department on one entry, while other nocturnal hospital coverage uses Hospital Medicine. That is inconsistent unit taxonomy.
“Reading Room” is used as the department for all imaging locations, even for ultrasound-technologist call and interventional radiology suite coverage. Those are not equivalent operational units.
Nuclear Medicine physicians are assigned “NM reads,” which is plausible, but the schedule does not show technologist coverage, authorized-user considerations, or modality/site qualifications. Those may exist elsewhere, so this is an omission rather than a definite error.
Missing operational fields

A real schedule of this scope would commonly need at least some of the following:

Assignment status such as draft, confirmed, changed, canceled, or published
Last-modified timestamp and modifying user
Start and end dates as well as times
Call tier, backup provider, escalation path, and handoff time
Provider contact or paging reference
Facility-specific privilege or eligibility confirmation
Absence, leave, conflict, or substitution indicators
Coverage requirement versus assigned coverage
Department approval or schedule owner
Time zone, particularly for teleradiology
Location or modality details more structured than free-text notes
Version history and change log

Their absence makes the workbook weak as an operational schedule, though it could still be a reporting extract.

Cross-file concerns
Carol E. Renauld is scheduled repeatedly, including an entry marked “see credentialing note,” but the schedule does not identify the note, restriction, or clearance status. That phrase creates a safety-relevant dependency with no actionable reference.
Several Winston providers previously had duplicate active roster records. The schedule keys only on NPI, so it avoids choosing between duplicate UUIDs but may fail if downstream scheduling requires a specific provider record.
The earlier policy says scheduling eligibility is controlled through OpenMRS attributes, but this schedule contains no eligibility-check date, exception indicator, or source-record reference.
The workbook spans only April 13 through May 4 while carrying a Q2 control number. That is not inherently incorrect, but “Q2” could imply a quarter-wide artifact unless the three-week window is deliberately identified as an extract.
Strongest double-take items
A purported hospital coverage schedule with only two ED shifts.
On-call entries without start or end times.
Overnight shifts without end dates.
Urgent Care specialty used for primary Emergency Medicine coverage without qualification context.
Physician, clinic, OR, imaging, and technologist assignments combined in one underspecified grid.
“See credentialing note” without a note identifier or disposition.
Heavy reliance on NPI as the scheduling identity rather than a facility/provider record identifier.

Structural and process problems

Night shifts have no end date. SCH-006 and SCH-060 run 19:00–07:00 with a single Shift_Date column. Any agent computing coverage on 04/14 has no way to know the 04/13 night shift covers it. SCH-018's 17:00–08:00 is a 15-hour block that matches no standard nocturnist pattern.

On-Call rows have no times at all — "—" in both columns, including SCH-024 labeled "24h in-house call". In-house call is a physical presence obligation and is always time-bounded.

Coverage_Type has no legend. Primary, Coverage, and On-Call are used inconsistently — weekend rounders are sometimes "Coverage" (SCH-031) and sometimes "Primary" (SCH-101 is "Coverage," SCH-105 "Primary" on the same weekend).

A technologist is on the physician coverage grid. SCH-068 schedules Marchetti (RDMS) for "Weekend US tech call" under a Provider_Individual_NPI column. Tech staffing lives in a separate departmental schedule, not the Medical Staff Office coverage grid — and she's one of the A09 exception providers.

Control number says Q2, window is three weeks. SCH-2026-Q2 covers 04/13–05/04. Also, 04/13/2026 as "Anchor" is not scheduling vocabulary; call sheets have effective dates and publication dates.
