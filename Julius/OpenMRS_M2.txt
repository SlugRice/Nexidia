## 1. How OpenMRS actually models each of these things

### 1.1 The four candidate vehicles

| Vehicle | Table(s) | What it is for | Value storage | Repeating? |

### 1.2 Verdict on your current modelling

**Your suspicion is correct.** Expressing "Coverage Type" and "Department" as Concepts with Obs bound to Encounters is wrong for administrative provider attributes, for three reasons:

- **Semantics.** An `obs` is an assertion *about a patient's health state or care*, recorded by a provider, at an encounter. "This provider's billing group NPI is 1384970293" is not an observation about a patient. A credentialing professional reading the environment will see obs-modelled NPIs as obviously synthetic.
- **Subject.** Your providers are modelled as patients purely so they can hold obs. That is a workaround, not a model. In OpenMRS a Provider needs a **Person**, not a **Patient**. Persons who are not patients are first-class.
- **Queryability.** Obs values for administrative fields are not visible in any provider-management UI. The agent would have to reason about a provider by reading their patient chart. That is not how anyone works.

**What to keep:** the two-week coverage calendar (Visit / Encounter / EncounterType / Location) is a defensible, if unusual, representation of on-call and coverage scheduling, and it is harmless. Keep it as *ambient realism* — it gives the environment depth and gives the agent irrelevant data to filter out. But sever it from the administrative layer entirely. Delete the "Coverage Type" and "Department" concepts and their obs rows, or retain them as scheduling colour only. Nothing scored should depend on them.

**What to change:** all nine crosswalk targets become **ProviderAttributeTypes**, not PersonAttributeTypes. Your crosswalk document currently says "OpenMRS Provider / Person Attribute targets", which is ambiguous but not wrong — it just needs to resolve to Provider.

## 2. Append-only history, effective dates, and authors

### 2.1 What OpenMRS gives you for free

Every attribute row carries `creator`, `date_created`, `changed_by`, `date_changed`, `voided`, `voided_by`, `date_voided`, `void_reason`, `uuid`. So **author** and **write timestamp** are native and per-value. That half of your document is accurate.

### 2.2 What it does not give you

There is **no effective-date column** on `provider_attribute` or `person_attribute`. There is no valid-from/valid-to. OpenMRS attributes are point-in-time current values with an audit trail, not a bitemporal store.

There are two things OpenMRS does instead, and you should know both:

- **Void-and-replace.** The standard correction pattern: void the old attribute with a `void_reason`, insert a new one. The old row remains in the table, readable, flagged `voided = 1`. This is *audit-preserving* but it is not append-only, because the prior value stops being a live value.
- **Repeating attributes.** If `max_occurs` is NULL, many non-voided attributes of one type coexist on one provider. This *is* genuinely append-only. The cost is that OpenMRS has no opinion about which one wins — "most recent by effective date" is a convention your scenario imposes, not something the platform enforces or the UI displays.

### 2.3 Recommendation

**Use repeating attributes and encode the effective date in the value, pipe-delimited.** This is the only way to get what your documents describe without writing a module.

Canonical value grammar, used for every dated attribute type:

```
<value>|<effective_date ISO-8601>|<authority_reference>
```

Examples:

```
1384970293|2026-03-03|BILLING-MDM-CUTOVER-2026-03
Suspended|2026-03-28|MEC-2026-03-28-014
Active|2026-04-05|GB-REINSTATE-2026-04-05-003
Hold|2026-04-13|NYSED-REG-LAPSE-2026-02-12
```

For the three boolean flags, the grammar extends to carry the clear date:

```
<state>|<effective_date>|<clear_date or NULL>|<authority_reference>
Pending|2026-04-13|NULL|CRED-EVIDENCE-SEARCH-2026-Q2
```

Why pipe-delimited rather than JSON: `value_reference` is a text column so JSON would fit, but a delimited value stays legible in the OpenMRS provider UI, which renders the raw `value_reference` for FreeText attributes. An agent reading the screen can parse it; a human evaluator can read it. JSON in that field looks like a bug.

**Enforce the grammar with `RegexValidatedTextDatatype`** on the NPI types so a malformed agent write fails loudly rather than silently corrupting the fixture.

### 2.4 The paired-attribute problem — fix this in the documents

`Clinical_Privilege_Scope` and `Clinical_Privilege_Effective_Date` as two separate repeating attribute types is **broken by design**. With two independent repeating collections there is nothing joining scope #2 to date #2. After two appends you have two scopes and two dates and no way to know which pairs with which.

**Collapse them into one attribute type** using the grammar above:

```
Clinical Privilege Scope = "Restricted — no inpatient admitting|2025-09-12|MEC-2025-09-12-007"
Clinical Privilege Scope = "Full active privileges|2026-02-18|GB-2026-02-18-002"
```

Then update the crosswalk to describe one target, `Clinical_Privilege_Scope`, whose value carries the effective date, and drop `Clinical_Privilege_Effective_Date` as a separate row — or redefine it as a *derived read* of the second segment. Otherwise task 10 is ambiguous: an agent that appends a scope but not a date, or vice versa, is neither right nor wrong.

### 2.5 Document wording to change

Replace the update-semantics section with something the platform can actually honour:

> Attribute types in this domain are configured as repeating (unbounded `maxOccurs`). A correction is recorded by **adding** a new attribute value; the prior value is left non-voided and remains a live row. Each value carries its effective date as the second pipe-delimited segment, and its author and write timestamp in the record's `creator` and `date_created` audit fields. The authoritative value for a type is the one with the latest effective date, ties broken by `date_created`. Voiding is reserved for values entered in error, not for superseded values.

That last sentence is important: it tells the agent when *not* to void, which is otherwise the obvious wrong move in tasks 8 and 10.

### 2.6 Option B, for completeness

Core OpenMRS does have one construct with native start/end dating and orthogonal dimensions: **Program enrolment / workflow / state** (`patient_program`, `patient_state` with `start_date` and `end_date`, `program_workflow_state`). Each program is a dimension; each workflow's states are mutually exclusive within it; states are dated and sequenced natively. That maps to your six status dimensions almost perfectly.

I do **not** recommend it, for two reasons: it only attaches to *patients*, which re-commits you to the provider-as-patient distortion; and using clinical program enrolment to model medical-staff appointment status would read as bizarre to anyone who knows the product. Mentioning it so you know the trade-off was considered.

## 3. Names, aliases, and voided duplicates

### 3.1 Name history

`person_name` holds **many rows per person**. Columns: `person_name_id`, `preferred` (bit), `person_id`, `prefix`, `given_name`, `middle_name`, `family_name_prefix`, `family_name`, `family_name2`, `family_name_suffix`, `degree`, plus the full audit/void set.

There is **no name-type column** in core OpenMRS. An alias or former name is simply an additional `person_name` row with `preferred = 0`. The current legal name is the row with `preferred = 1`. Exactly one row per person should be preferred.

So for your 17 former-name aliases and for Dr Whitcombe: one extra `person_name` row each, `preferred = 0`, `date_created` set to the acquisition-integration window. Whitcombe gets:

```
preferred=1  Janet    Whitcombe
preferred=0  Elizabeth Constantine
```

This is the whole fix for task 7. The committee minutes name resolves against a non-preferred name on the same person, and the same person carries NPI 1132083712 on `provider.identifier`. A competent agent searching OpenMRS for "Elizabeth Constantine" will land on Whitcombe, because OpenMRS name search matches non-preferred names by default.

**Do not** model former names as separate person records. That would collide with task 2, where separate person records *are* the duplicate signal. The whole point of task 2's 17-vs-5 discrimination is that aliases are one person with two names and duplicates are two persons with one name each.

### 3.2 Voiding a duplicate person

Two mechanisms:

- **Void.** Set `person.voided = 1`, `voided_by`, `date_voided`, `void_reason`. The `patient` row is voided alongside. The record disappears from normal search but is retrievable by UUID. This is what your task describes and what I recommend, because it is a single auditable act the agent performs by UUID with a reason.
- **Merge.** `PatientService.mergePatients(preferred, notPreferred)` moves encounters, obs, identifiers and names onto the survivor, voids the loser, and writes a `PersonMergeLog` row. Richer, but it auto-migrates the loser's identifiers onto the survivor, which would **do task 2's cross-reference step for the agent**. Avoid merge for this scenario.

### 3.3 What a duplicate pair must differ on

Today every field would be identical, so you are right that the task is unanswerable. The pair needs at least these discriminators, all visible in the OpenMRS UI:

| Field | Pre-close record | Post-acquisition record |
|---|---|---|
| `person.uuid` | distinct | distinct |
| `person.date_created` | 2025-02-18 (wave 1, pre-close) | 2025-04-22 (wave 2, post-acquisition) |
| `person.creator` | `winston.migration` | `coopablue.integration` |
| Patient identifier type | `Winston Legacy Provider ID` | `Coopa Blue Provider ID` |
| Identifier value | `WIN-04412` style | `CB-10233` style |
| Identifier location | the originating imaging site | Coopa Blue Regional Medical Center |
| `patient_identifier.preferred` | 1 on its own record | 1 on its own record |
| `provider.identifier` (the NPI) | **same on both** | **same on both** |

The shared NPI is the duplicate signal. The differing internal identifier, creation date and creator are the "which one is earlier / which is canonical" signal. The retired internal identifier the agent must carry across is the pre-close record's `WIN-#####` value, which it writes onto the survivor as `Retired Internal Identifier Cross-Reference = "WIN-04412|2026-04-13|DEDUP-2026-04-13"`.

Note the identifier types must be configured **non-unique** (`patient_identifier_type.unique_over_location = false` / not required-unique), otherwise the loader will reject the duplicate pairs.

### 3.4 Person vs Patient — a decision you should make deliberately

Ideally providers are Persons with Provider rows and no Patient row at all. That is correct OpenMRS and correct realism: clinicians are not patients of their own hospital by virtue of being on staff.

If your build pipeline can only create Persons via a patient loader, keep the Patient rows but understand you have accepted a visible modelling wart. If the pipeline is being regenerated anyway, **fix it**: split `Patient.csv` into `Person.csv` and make `Provider.csv` the only thing that references it. This is the single largest authenticity improvement available to you.

## 4. Per-payer enrolment values

A provider has many payer enrolments, each with its own organisational NPI, effective date, and enrolment status. OpenMRS core has no payer entity and no enrolment entity.

Three ways to hold it:

| Approach | How | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **One attribute type per payer** — `Payer Enrollment Org NPI — Empire BlueCross`, `… — MVP Health Care`, etc. | Each type `max_occurs = NULL`, value `<NPI>\|<effective_date>\|<enrollment_ref>` | **Recommended** for a fixed roster of 5–7 payers. Values stay atomic, each payer is independently queryable and independently writable, and the task-9 target is unambiguous: one named type on one provider holding the stale value. |
| **One repeating type with the payer inside the value** — `Payer Enrollment Org NPI` = `Empire BlueCross\|1342281247\|2025-06-01\|ENR-88213` | Single type, unlimited occurrences | Workable and scales past a fixed roster, but the agent must parse to find the right payer, and a careless append can silently create a second live row for the same payer. Use only if your payer list must be open-ended. |
| **Custom module with a real enrolment table** | Out of scope | Correct in production, wrong for a seeded eval fixture. |

Go with per-payer attribute types. Seed a roster of six payers so the environment has texture: two commercial, one Blues plan, one Medicaid managed care, Medicare, and one delegated-credentialing arrangement (which is the payer that task 5's missing roster belongs to).

For task 9: Dr Vantrease (1473953671) carries `Payer Enrollment Org NPI — <one named payer>` = `1342281247|2025-06-01|ENR-…`. Every other provider's payer-enrolment attributes carry `1384970293`. The agent appends `1384970293|2026-04-13|…` to Vantrease's record for that payer only.

## 6. Plausibility review

Things a credentialing professional would notice.

**Solid as written**

- Two hospitals at 150 and 65 beds with 249 credentialed providers is a normal ratio for a community health system. Credentialed staff routinely outnumber what bed count alone suggests, because it includes consulting, courtesy and telemedicine staff.
- New York genuinely uses a licence-plus-*registration* model, with registration renewed on a recurring cycle. "Registration lapsed 12 February 2026" is exactly right, and better than the generic "licence expired" most synthetic scenarios use. Keep it.
- A subsidiary continuing to bill under its own Type 2 NPI through an integration period, then being cut over, is a real and common pattern.
- Governing body reinstatement following a medical executive committee recommendation, with a lag before it reaches downstream systems, is the single most realistic detail in the whole scenario.
- Five duplicates out of 249 from a two-wave migration is a believable rate. So is 17 former names out of 249.
- Delegated credentialing rosters and roster-submission gaps are a genuine and frequent post-acquisition failure.

**Would raise an eyebrow**

1. **Providers as patients.** Covered in §3.4. Biggest issue. Fix if you can.
2. **An EMR as the system of record for credentialing.** In a real US health system this data lives in a credentialing platform — symplr, MD-Staff, Verity, IntelliCred — and the EMR holds at most a thin provider directory. Consider adding one line to the crosswalk acknowledging that OpenMRS is the *operational provider directory* and that the medical staff office system is upstream. That single sentence makes the whole environment read correctly.
3. **One `Billing_Group_NPI` per provider.** Real providers bill under multiple groups and multiple TINs simultaneously — hospital employed, faculty practice, locum arrangement. A single-valued billing group is a simplification. It is a defensible one for an eval, but if you want more authenticity, make the value carry a TIN segment too.
4. **Retiring an organisational NPI.** NPIs are deactivated in NPPES, and an organisational NPI is normally deactivated only when the legal entity ceases to exist. In an acquisition where the subsidiary survives as a legal entity, the far more common situation is that the NPI stays *active in NPPES* but is *removed from billing configuration*. Your documents say "retired from billing systems", which is the right phrasing. Make sure no document says the NPI was deactivated at NPPES, because those are different acts with different consequences.
5. **"Quarterly attestation."** Ongoing monitoring in real credentialing is usually continuous or monthly for exclusion and sanction screening (OIG LEIE, SAM, state Medicaid exclusion lists), with expirables tracked by expiry date rather than a quarterly cycle. A quarterly *attestation* — provider self-attestation of no material change — does exist but is less common than quarterly *monitoring*. Either rename it to match the monitoring convention or add a sentence establishing it as a Coopa Blue internal policy.
6. **Missing identifiers.** A credentialing record with NPI but no TIN, no DEA, no state licence number, no CAQH ID and no board certification status looks thin. I have added `State License Number` and `Tax Identification Number` to the attribute type list. Consider also CAQH ID and DEA — even unused by any task, they make the record look like a record.
7. **No expirables.** Real provider records are dominated by dated expirables: licence, DEA, malpractice coverage, board certification, BLS/ACLS. Seeding a handful with future expiry dates costs nothing and adds substantial texture.
8. **Winston providers and hospital privileges.** Outpatient imaging radiologists from an acquired group would not automatically hold admitting privileges at either hospital. If your coverage calendar has Winston-origin providers on hospital on-call rotations, that is a small inconsistency. Worth a scan.
9. **Voiding versus merging a duplicate.** In a real medical staff office the resolution of a duplicate provider record is a *merge* with a documented survivorship decision, not a void. Voiding is fine for the eval because it is a cleaner discrete act, but expect a domain expert to note it. One sentence in the scenario documents — that Coopa Blue policy is to void and cross-reference rather than merge, to preserve the pre-close record intact for audit — resolves it entirely and is itself plausible.
