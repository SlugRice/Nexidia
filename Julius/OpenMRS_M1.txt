Bottom line

The current seed treats providers as patients and stores administrative workforce data as clinical observations. That is not an idiomatic OpenMRS model.

Use:

person for provider identity and demographics.
person_name for current and former names.
provider for the provider role and provider identifier.
provider_attribute_type and provider_attribute for simple provider-level administrative values.
A purpose-built credentialing/enrollment history entity for dated, dimensional statuses and per-payer enrollment.
Do not use patient_identifier for NPIs unless these people genuinely need patient records.
Do not use obs, encounters, or clinical concepts for credentialing, billing affiliation, appointment status, employment, or payer enrollment.

OpenMRS distinguishes Person, Patient, and Provider. A provider may link to a person, while patient identifiers belong to the patient role. Providers also have their own identifier and typed provider attributes.

2. Append-only history is not native attribute behavior

Your documented history semantics exceed ordinary OpenMRS person/provider attributes.

Core attribute rows have system audit metadata such as creator and creation timestamp, but that is not the same as:

business effective date
clear date
status dimension
source authority
payer
governing-body decision date
“latest by effective date, then write timestamp”

The ordinary attribute model is fundamentally typed name/value metadata. It does not by itself implement your authoritative-value algorithm.

Recommended document correction

Replace:

A correction is recorded as a new attribute value carrying its own effective date and author.

With:

Credentialing and provider-administration history is stored as immutable Provider Administrative History records. Each record identifies the provider, status dimension, business-effective date, recorded timestamp, recording user, source, and value. Corrections are appended as new records. The governing record within a dimension is the non-voided record having the latest effective date, with recorded timestamp as the tie-breaker.

Then distinguish:

Current snapshot provider attributes
 Used by OpenMRS screens and writable agent tasks.

Administrative history records
 Used for append-only lineage and determining the current state.

When an agent makes a correction, the application should:

insert a history row
update or add the current snapshot attribute
never delete the older history row

If you store multiple ordinary provider attributes of the same type, you still need custom code to select the authoritative one. Do not claim that OpenMRS natively applies your effective-date rule.

6. Operational authenticity issues
Clinically or operationally questionable

Providers represented as patients
 This is the largest authenticity problem.

“Old Identification Number” used for NPI
 An NPI should be explicitly typed as NPI, preferably Type 1 versus Type 2.

NPI declared numeric
 Model NPIs as fixed-length identifier strings with validation. They are not numbers used for calculation.

Billing group NPI directly on every individual provider
 Plausible as a denormalized operational snapshot, but real billing affiliation often varies by legal entity, site, service, payer, tax arrangement, and effective period.

One payer enrollment NPI without a payer key
 Not viable. The payer must be part of the enrollment record’s identity.

Appointment, privilege, credentialing, employment, and scheduling treated as one status
 Your documents correctly say these are separate dimensions. Preserve that.

Boolean pending flags without lifecycle data
 If the scenario requires effective and clear dates, a bare Boolean is insufficient. Use a dated history record plus an optional current Boolean snapshot.

Privilege scope paired with a separate effective-date attribute
 Two independent attributes can become mismatched. A single privilege-grant record containing scope and effective date is safer and more authentic.

Voiding instead of merging duplicate persons
 Possible for a scored synthetic workflow, but production duplicate resolution usually requires careful transfer or reconciliation of dependent records, not only setting voided = true.

Organizational NPI described as “retired”
 If 1342281247 remains active for Winston as a legal organization but is no longer permitted for these billing workflows, call it “legacy for Coopa Blue billing” or “decommissioned from enterprise billing use.” “Retired NPI” implies an NPPES status that your scenario has not established.

Final recommended architecture

Use ordinary provider attributes only as searchable current-state snapshots. Put all dated, append-only credentialing, appointment, privilege, hold, and payer-enrollment data into dedicated administrative history entities. That gives the tasks deterministic findings while keeping the OpenMRS application behavior believable.
