Overall verdict

These look like synthetic test datasets rather than extracts from mature provider-record systems. The largest tells are unrealistic credential identifiers, uniform date construction, conflated credentialing concepts, and unusually clean, comprehensive population patterns.

Highest-confidence concerns

NY license and registration identifiers are conflated
 The roster labels values such as NP-14698, PA-21553, and T-088204 as NY_License_Number. The recredentialing log then places the same values under NYSED_Registration_Number. These look like fabricated internal identifiers, not realistic New York professional license or registration identifiers.

Imaging technologist credentials are modeled incorrectly
 ARRT-R, ARRT-MR, ARRT-CT, and RDMS are certification credentials, not New York professional-license types. The files then assign these technologists Type 1 NPIs and T- registration numbers inside the same NYSED framework used for physicians, NPs, and PAs. Imaging or credentialing personnel would likely question that immediately.

Registration dates appear algorithmically generated
 The physician registration issue and expiration dates repeatedly differ by about two years, while nonphysician dates repeatedly differ by about three years. This mirrors the incorrect policy rule in the prior batch and is too uniformly constructed. Real NY registration records would contain irregularities from initial registration, late renewal, inactive periods, restoration, and differing profession rules.

A “December recredentialing log” contains almost the entire provider population
 The log contains 245 rows, while the roster contains 254 rows including duplicate migration records. A single monthly recredentialing cycle covering essentially the whole organization would be unusual unless this is expressly an annual census or reconciliation rather than a normal recredentialing cohort. Most organizations stagger recredentialing based on credentialing anniversary or expiration.

Credentialing completion and registration status are mixed together
 The columns combine recredentialing cycle completion, primary-source verification, registration dates, and a generic Status. Several entries show PSV as complete but status as Renewal-in-progress; others retain older July entries as pending while a December entry for the same person is complete. A mature log would ordinarily separate provider credentialing status, individual credential status, workflow status, and exception disposition more clearly.

Major plausibility problems
Raw Excel serial dates are exposed. Values such as 46009, 46369, and 45993 appear instead of formatted dates. A working spreadsheet can store dates this way internally, but a distributed credentialing log would normally display recognizable dates.
Recred_Cycle_Completed mixes dates and status words. It contains serial dates, Pending, and blanks. That violates ordinary field typing and makes reporting unreliable.
PSV_Status is unusually binary. Nearly every December record is simply Complete. Real PSV tracking typically operates at the credential-item level, with source, verification date, expiration, discrepancy status, verifier, and supporting evidence.
The generic Status column is ambiguous. Current, Under Review, and Renewal-in-progress describe different conceptual dimensions.
Old pending rows remain open after later completion. Multiple practitioners have a July row that remains Pending or Under Review and a December row marked complete. If these are separate workflow episodes, the July episode should ordinarily have a closed, superseded, canceled, or rolled-forward disposition.
Terminated practitioners remain Current. The notes state that employment terminated, but the generic status remains Current. That may mean credential verification is current, but the field does not say so. It invites exactly the cross-status confusion the policy claims to prevent.
Renewal entries contain implausible wording. One entry says CE hours verified for an NYSED renewal. Continuing-education requirements vary by profession and credential, so this should not appear as a generic physician-registration milestone without greater specificity.
The log is too sparse for a genuine recredentialing control. It lacks application receipt, sanctions queries, exclusions, malpractice history, board status, hospital affiliations, work history, peer review, attestation, committee date, approval date, next cycle, assigned analyst, aging, and exception category.
No source evidence is identified. Complete does not indicate what was checked, where, when, or by whom.
Provider records are represented one row per cycle rather than one row per credential item. This is possible as a summary log, but then calling it the primary recredentialing tracking log overstates what it can evidence.
A01_Master_Provider_Roster.xlsx
Major plausibility problems
HR owns the “Master Provider Roster.” Employment data reasonably belongs to HR, but NPI, license, specialty, migration identity, practice-site assignment, and operational provider identity commonly span HR, Credentialing, Medical Staff Services, IT, and payer enrollment. Calling an HR spreadsheet the master source would draw questions.
No credentialing or privileging status appears. For a master provider roster, the file lacks appointment status, privilege status, credentialing dates, effective/end dates, payer enrollment, taxonomy, organizational NPI, TIN relationship, provider type, and next recredentialing date.
NP and PA are used as credentials. These describe practitioner categories more than precise professional credentials. Experienced users might expect values such as NP specialty certification, PA-C, RN/license information, or a separate provider-type field.
Specialty is presented as one universal field. Real providers may have taxonomy, board specialty, credentialed specialty, employed department, clinical service, and scheduling specialty that do not always match.
Terminated and leave records have no effective dates. A current-state status without start, termination, or leave dates is inadequate for historical operational use.
Alias data is suspiciously tidy. Former names appear as complete first-and-last-name strings in one flat field, with no alias type, effective dates, verification, or multiple-name support.
Migration duplicates remain active through April 2026. The roster contains five apparent Pre-close/Post-acquisition pairs, both marked active, even though the prior SOP says reconciliation must leave exactly one active row and must occur in a single work session. This is a direct cross-file operational contradiction.
The new-hire exclusion note is artificial. A roster dated April 13, 2026 expressly excludes providers onboarded March 12 pending the “next scheduled roster refresh.” A month-long lag in the declared master provider roster would be operationally problematic and potentially unsafe if the roster supports credentialing, scheduling, or access.
N/A is used where prior documents said the migration field was blank. This confirms inconsistent null handling across the document set.

The cross-file skeleton actually holds up here: Winston-origin resolves to exactly 24 distinct providers across 29 rows, the five Pre-close/Post-acquisition pairs give you 254 rows / 249 distinct exactly as A05 claims, Migration_Wave really is column N, Onboarded_Date 45719 really is 03/03/2025, and the Leave/Terminated flags in A01 match the A02 notes. That's better internal engineering than the policy layer. The problems are in the licensure data and in the recredentialing process itself.

The licensure columns don't survive contact with New York

Every registration term is exactly two years. I checked issue-to-expiry across credentials: MDs, NPs, PAs, and ARRT technologists all come in at 730–731 days. A03 §3.3(b) says non-physician NYSED professions are on a three-year cycle. Every non-MD row in the log contradicts the policy that governs it.

License number and registration number are the same value. A01 calls 244040 a NY_License_Number; A02 calls the identical value a NYSED_Registration_Number. A03 §2.3 and §3.1 are built entirely on these being distinct instruments — the enduring license versus the periodic registration. The data collapses the distinction the policy exists to preserve. In reality the registration is evidenced by a registration certificate with its own period, not a second number.

Prefixed license numbers aren't a thing. NP-14698, PA-21553, T-088204. NYSED issues plain six-digit license numbers across all professions. Also, NY doesn't license "NP" as a standalone license — nurse practitioners hold an RN license plus NP certification, so the number on file would be the RN license.

Eight technologist rows carry credentials that can't exist in New York. Radiologic technologists are licensed and registered by the Department of Health, Bureau of Environmental Radiation Protection — not NYSED — and only in Radiography, Therapy, and Nuclear Medicine. So:

Vance and Boucher (ARRT-MR) — MRI uses no ionizing radiation and isn't a licensed category in NY
Rivera and Marchetti (RDMS) — diagnostic medical sonographers aren't state-licensed in NY at all
Trask and Restrepo (ARRT-CT) — CT is a post-primary ARRT certification; the NY license would be Radiography
Brennan and Rivera (ARRT-R) — correctly licensed, but through DOH, so the column header is wrong

All eight sit under a NYSED_Registration_Number column with a T- prefix. This is the same hole as A03 §3.3(b), now instantiated 8 times in data.

The lapse scenario the corpus is built around never actually occurs

The earliest Registration_Expiry_Date anywhere in A02 is 2026-02-12. The four Renewal-in-progress rows — the ones that look like planted anomalies — expire 04/22/2026, 05/05/2026, 05/08/2026, and 05/11/2026. As of the log's own 12/2025 date, and as of A01's 04/13/2026 as-of date, nothing is expired.

That breaks two things. A03 §5.1's inserted paragraph — Status of "Current" doesn't override a passed Registration_Expiry_Date — has no row to fire on. And A03 §4's 30-day advance-warning threshold is contradicted by renewal packets filed four to five months early, which no one does.

Related: A03 §4.1 states the tracking log identifies practitioners expiring within 30 days. A02 has no such column, flag, or derived field.

Process realism in A02

The whole panel was recredentialed in one cycle. Recred_Cycle_Start is 12/2025 for all 245 rows, with completions clustered between 2025-11-01 and 2025-12-30. NCQA runs on a 36-month cycle, staggered by each provider's prior credentialing date — you'd expect roughly a twelfth of the panel per month, not 237 files in sixty days. There's no per-provider due date column that would drive staggering.

Completions fall on weekends. Serial 45962 is Saturday 11/01/2025 and 45963 is Sunday 11/02/2025; both carry completed files. So does 46019, Sunday 12/28.

Last_Updated frequently precedes Recred_Cycle_Completed. Row 17 completes on 46019 and was last updated 46012; row 23 completes 46018, updated 46013. A record can't be stamped as updated before the event it records.

Terminated providers were recredentialed and marked Current. Pratt, Ramsey, Logan, and Watkins are Terminated in A01 but carry completed 12/2025 cycles with Status "Current". Real practice is to close the file at separation, not run the cycle.

Twelve roster providers are missing from the log — Ellis, Ortega, Larson, Tucker, Norquist R., Trask B., Emerson K., Bailey, Woodard, Villegas, Osborne R., Porter — with no cycle assignment or exclusion reason anywhere.

The eight July/December pairs create a false-positive trap. Eight NPIs appear twice in A02 as cycle rows. A06 §5.2 defines a duplicate as "two rows sharing an NPI." Anything applying that test to A02 rather than A01 gets eight wrong answers.

What a recredentialing log can't omit

A02 has no Credentials Committee approval date, no governing body approval date, no NPDB query date, no OIG LEIE / SAM / OMIG screening date, no malpractice insurance carrier or expiry, no board certification or expiry, no DEA, no taxonomy code, no CAQH ID, and no next-recredentialing-due date. The committee approval date is the most conspicuous — the entire purpose of a recred tracking log is tracking files to committee and board sign-off, and A03 §1.5 makes the governing body the only grantor. The log tracks a cycle that terminates nowhere.

A01 likewise has no termination date, no multi-site capability (one Practice_Site per provider), and no DEA or board data.

Generation tells

All NPIs pass the checksum again — I spot-checked fifteen across both files, all valid. Third file set in a row.

Alias rows: I count 16, not the 17 that A05 §5 and A06 §5.4 both assert. Worth recounting on your side, but if it's 16, two controlled documents state a population the source file doesn't support.

The alias values are the wrong shape. Every one changes both the first and last name — Whitcombe Janet L. → "Elizabeth Constantine," Ackerman Roxanne C. → "Vivian Wexler". Real former-name fields are dominated by maiden names, where the surname changes and the given name doesn't. And five male providers carry female former names: Odom Tobias → "Millicent Radley," Maldonado Karl L. → "Theodora Ellsworth," Shaw Carl W. → "Josephine Marston," Merritt Nathaniel D. → "Beatrice Merriam," Waters Blake → "Cordelia Prescott." The names are also uniformly period-costume — Adelaide, Winifred, Prudence, Genevieve, Cordelia — against a roster of ordinary contemporary names.

Migration_Wave is the literal string "N/A." A05 §4 says the field is intentionally blank for non-Winston providers. As written, an agent filtering on blank finds nothing, and Gibbs's four blank-tagged new hires in A44 would be indistinguishable from an untagged error.

The roster is NPI-sorted with scattered exceptions — 1991703426 at row 34, 1488171525 at row 41, 1220244960 at row 19, 1755882630 at row 36. Those are precisely the rows where a value looks substituted after sorting. A dozen or so of these pattern as post-generation edits.

Dates are stored as raw serials, unformatted. In a file a credentialing director actually maintains, those columns are date-formatted.

One last cross-file note: the six A09 exception providers — Bellinger, Marchetti, Halloran, Boucher, Sullivan, Ashworth — all appear in A02 as Complete/Current with no flag, note, or hold of any kind, which is exactly what A08 M-005/M-006 say should be attached.
